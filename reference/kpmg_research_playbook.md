# KPMG-Standard Research Playbook

This is the **mandatory** research methodology for every engagement produced by `executive-market-research`. It mirrors the actual approach used by KPMG, Deloitte, McKinsey, and BCG when producing a feasibility study or market entry assessment.

**Read this in full before starting any new report.**

## The 7 Golden Rules (non-negotiable)

| # | Rule | Application |
|---|------|-------------|
| 1 | **Triangulation** | Every critical number must come from **at least 3 independent sources**. If you can't get 3, lower the confidence label and disclose. |
| 2 | **Freshness** | Prefer data from the last 18 months. Anything older than 24 months must be flagged. State the publication date for every cited number. |
| 3 | **Multilingual** | Search in **English + the country's primary languages** (e.g., French + Arabic for Algeria, Spanish for LATAM, Portuguese for Brazil). Many local sources publish only in the national language. |
| 4 | **Official first** | Government / international organisation sources (World Bank, IMF, UN, national stats office) **before** commercial market-research firms. |
| 5 | **Benchmarking** | Always compare the target country to 2–3 peer markets (e.g., Algeria vs. Morocco + Tunisia + Egypt for North Africa industrial). |
| 6 | **ESG mandatory** | Every report covers Environmental, Social, and Governance dimensions — even when not explicitly requested. |
| 7 | **Primary research simulation** | List 5–10 interview questions you would ask key informants, even if the engagement is desk-research only. Demonstrates depth. |

## The 5-Phase Research Funnel

### Phase 0 — Scoping (5 minutes)
Before any search, write down on paper:
- **Geography**: country + region (e.g., "Algeria + North Africa export markets")
- **Product / sector**: precise definition + relevant HS codes
- **Time horizon**: historical (5y) + forecast (5–10y)
- **Dimensions to cover**: market size, demand, supply, prices, competition, regulation, costs, ESG, risk

### Phase 1 — Quick orientation (10–15 minutes)
Run rapid searches in 3 languages to get the lay of the land:
- "[product] market [country] 2025"
- "[product] industry report [country]"
- "[country] [product] regulation"
- "[product] [country] feasibility study"

Capture: key dates, headline numbers, names of organisations and projects, terms specific to the local market.

### Phase 2 — Official sources (deepest, most credible)
Hit these in order:

1. **National statistical office** of the target country
2. **Central bank** of the target country
3. **Customs authority** of the target country
4. **World Bank Open Data** + WB country page
5. **IMF DataMapper** + WEO database
6. **UN Comtrade** for trade flows by HS code
7. **WTO Tariff Download Facility** for current tariffs
8. **OECD Stat** if the country is OECD or partner
9. Sector-specific UN agencies (FAO, UNIDO, WHO, IRENA, etc.)

### Phase 3 — Specialised market data
Use these for sector-level depth:

1. **Statista** — broadest starting point; sector + country combos
2. **Euromonitor Passport** — consumer goods specialty
3. **IBISWorld** — industry-level structural reports
4. **Fitch Solutions / BMI** — country + industry monthly briefs
5. **EIU (Economist Intelligence Unit)** — country risk + scenarios
6. **Mordor Intelligence**, **Grand View Research**, **6Wresearch**, **Frost & Sullivan** — purchasable reports
7. **CRU Group**, **S&P Global Commodity Insights** — commodities
8. **Gartner / Forrester / IDC** — tech, IT, digital transformation
9. **Mintel** — consumer goods

### Phase 4 — Financial & news cross-check
Validate and enrich with:

1. **Bloomberg / Reuters / Eikon** — real-time financial + news (paid)
2. **Financial Times** — international business
3. **The Economist** — analytical pieces, country reviews
4. **Local high-quality press** — examples:
   - North Africa: Jeune Afrique, Algérie Presse Service, Maghreb Émergent
   - GCC: The National (UAE), Arab News (KSA), Gulf Times (Qatar)
   - Africa: African Business, BusinessDay (Nigeria)
5. **Sector trade press**: e.g., MEED for Middle East projects, World Steel Association for steel, ICIS for petrochemicals
6. **Company filings**: 10-K (US, SEC EDGAR), 20-F (foreign filers), local stock-exchange filings, annual reports
7. **Consultancy reports**: McKinsey Global Institute, BCG insights, Deloitte Insights, PwC, EY — usually free, watch for advocacy/bias

### Phase 5 — Academic & technical (for deep / niche topics)
- **Google Scholar** — peer-reviewed, technical feasibility, life-cycle analyses
- **JSTOR**, **ABI/INFORM (ProQuest)** — through university libraries
- **SSRN** — working papers, especially on emerging markets
- **National research institutes** — e.g., CREAD (Algeria), CGEM (Morocco)

## The triangulation table (mandatory for every report)

For every headline statistic in the report, maintain a small audit table:

| Statistic | Source 1 | Source 2 | Source 3 | Confidence |
|-----------|----------|----------|----------|-----------|
| Market size 2024 | UN Comtrade | ONS | Mordor Intelligence | High |
| CAGR 2019–2024 | KPMG analysis | EIU | World Steel Association | High |
| Top exporters | UN Comtrade | Trade Map | Customs Algeria | High |

Don't include this table in the deliverable — keep it in your research log so you can defend any number under interrogation.

## Search query templates

When orienting on a new topic, use these query patterns:

```
"[product] market size [country] [year]"
"[product] industry report [country]"
"[product] CAGR forecast"
"[country] [HS code] imports"
"[country] [sector] regulation"
"[product] competitor analysis [country]"
"[country] [product] tender announcement"
"[product] [country] feasibility study filetype:pdf"
"[country] [product] consumer behavior"
```

## Anti-patterns

- ❌ Quoting Wikipedia as the primary source
- ❌ "Market size estimated at $X billion (Source: industry estimates)"
- ❌ Citing aggregator screenshots without verifying the underlying source
- ❌ Translating English consumer behaviour insights to a non-English market
  without local validation
- ❌ Reporting historical data >3 years old as "current"
- ❌ Ignoring grey-market or informal-sector flows when they're material
- ❌ Citing only a single consultancy report — they have advocacy bias
- ❌ Missing the local-language search step

## What "good" looks like

A KPMG-grade report has:

- 30+ unique sources cited in the bibliography
- 3+ sources triangulated for every headline number
- Data primarily from 2023–2026
- A peer-country benchmark in at least 3 sections
- An ESG section even if brief
- 5–10 simulated primary-research interview questions
- Charts that are findings, not labels
- A risk register tied to specific data points
- Recommendations specific enough to action this quarter
